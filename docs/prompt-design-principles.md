# Prompt Design Principles

## A practical prompt anatomy

A reusable prompt usually contains the following parts:

1. **Objective:** What must be accomplished?
2. **Context:** What information is relevant?
3. **Inputs:** What material should be analyzed or transformed?
4. **Constraints:** What must or must not happen?
5. **Process:** Which reasoning or verification steps are required?
6. **Output:** What structure, level of detail, or format is expected?
7. **Evaluation:** How will the result be judged?

Not every prompt needs every part. Include only what improves task clarity or evaluation.

## Design checklist

- Is the objective specific and observable?
- Is the audience clear?
- Is the supplied context relevant and sufficient?
- Are unknowns and assumptions handled explicitly?
- Are risky or out-of-scope actions excluded?
- Is the requested output easy to inspect?
- Is there a verification step for important claims?
- Can the prompt be reused without hidden context?

## Iteration method

Start with a baseline, record the result, identify the most important weakness, and revise one variable. Keep the test input and evaluation criteria stable when comparing versions. This makes it easier to understand why a result changed.
